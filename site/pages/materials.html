01.1 / WHY MICROGRAVITY?

Change the environment.
Change what can form.

↓ · ↓

Less sedimentation

Particles and growing crystals settle less. That can open a longer window for suspension and self-assembly.

↻ → ·

Different transport

Reduced buoyancy-driven flow can make diffusion more important. Surface-tension flows and forced mixing can still dominate.

◇ → ◈

New structures to test

Changes in growth can affect size distributions, crystal forms and defects. Better functional performance must be measured separately.

Microgravity is a process condition, not a guarantee of improvement. Radiation, launch loads, thermal history and return introduce additional constraints. Protein-crystal study ↗ · Colloidal-crystal study ↗

01.5 / HOW WE FIND THE WINNERS

From image to insight.
From insight to proof.

01 / COLLECT

Traceable flight data

NASA PSI images, process telemetry and ground controls. Keep sample, run and condition identities intact.

02 / MEASURE

Validated features

Segment crystals and growth fronts. Measure size, defects and texture against manually reviewed labels.

03 / COMPARE

Independent batches

Estimate effects and uncertainty at the batch level. Avoid counting adjacent image frames as independent experiments.

04 / QUALIFY

Functional outcomes

Test viscosity, selectivity, cycling or optical loss after return. Retain only the advantages the application can use.

Current baseline: 12 NASA DSI images passed access and image-quality checks. This verifies the pipeline, not a material advantage. Engaging can scale validated extraction across runs.

01.6 / BIG DATA, NEXT

From archive
to flight manifest.

The archive tells us where to look. Our own flights will produce the data that tells us what works. This is how the evidence base grows.

01 / REPROCESS

One pipeline for every space-grown crystal.

Link each microgravity PDB entry to its raw diffraction images where they are public, then reprocess flight and ground crystals with the same software. This removes the beamline and era differences that blur archive comparisons.

02 / RECORD

Every batch becomes a data point.

Each foundry cartridge writes a provenance record: recipe, thermal history, images, residual acceleration and return timeline. Matched Earth controls use the same schema.

03 / LEARN

Choose the next flight with a model.

Join in-situ images to post-return assays (viscosity, selectivity, optical loss) and train models that predict which recipes benefit from orbit. Active learning picks the most informative next batch.

04 / READ

Turn the literature into records.

Extract conditions and outcomes from decades of microgravity papers into structured evidence records with links, reviewed by a scientist before use.
