## 1. Launch scale and the SpaceX effect (`launch-scale.json`)

**Data.** Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT), https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/ (CC-BY), snapshot "Updated 2026 Oct 24 1902:29". Files used: `launch.tsv` (76,014 launches of all kinds, 13.8 MB), the derived per-payload orbital `launchlog.tsv` (31,980 payload rows, 5.3 MB), and every object catalog (`satcat*`, `auxcat`, `lprcat`, `tmpcat`, `csocat`; 90,645 objects used for the mass lookup). In total 11 files, 200,435 records and 44.5 MB. The SHA-256 of every file is in the JSON `datasets[].notes`.

**Method.**
1. Orbital launch attempt = GCAT `LaunchCode` starting `O` (orbital) or `D` (deep space), with second letter `S` (success) or `F` (failure). `OE` (failed before ignition) and `OA` (aborted, no launch) are excluded.
2. SpaceX = launch agency `SPX` (who flew the rocket, not who owns the payload).
3. Payloads per year = launchlog rows on successful orbital/deep-space launches. Starlink = name starting "Starlink".
4. Mass to orbit = sum of the GCAT payload `Mass` (launch mass, kg), joined by JCAT. Coverage is reported per year (100% of 2025 payloads have a mass, but 15% of the 2025 mass is flagged as a GCAT estimate). The autogenerated `OrbPay` column is kept only as a cross-check.
5. Rideshare = launches named `Transporter-N` or `Bandwagon-N`; payloads per mission = launchlog rows with that launch designation.
6. Starship = every launch with `LV_Type` Starship*. "Integrated flights" excludes the 8 atmospheric hops of 2020–21.

**Results.**
- 7,414 orbital launch attempts from 1957 to 24 Oct 2026. 2025 had **329**, 3.78× the 87 of 2015.
- SpaceX flew **170 of 329 (51.7%)** in 2025 and carried **85.0% of the 4,570 payloads** that reached orbit (3,193 were Starlink).
- Payload mass placed in orbit in 2025: **3,096 t** (84.8% on SpaceX launches), 10.3× 2015 (301 t).
- 2026 to 24 Oct: 252 attempts (122 SpaceX) vs 250 (139 SpaceX) in the same window of 2025. Growth has paused this year in GCAT's count, and SpaceX's count is lower.
- 23 Transporter/Bandwagon missions carried **1,742 catalogued payloads**: median 74 per mission, range 4 (Bandwagon-3) to 145 (Transporter-1), with up to 63 distinct owners per launch.
- Starship: **14 integrated flights**, 10 coded successful. GCAT puts flights 4–14 (11 flights, including two failures) in its orbital category; the successful ones have perigees of −40 to +8 km, i.e. at the edge of orbit.

**Limitations.** 2026 is partial. Launch counts follow GCAT definitions, and other trackers differ by a few launches a year on borderline cases. Masses are launch masses, often estimates or nominal for classified payloads, and they exclude upper stages. SpaceX share counts the launch provider. Recent rideshare missions may not be fully catalogued. Above all, this is capacity *up*; capacity *down* is the bottleneck (section 2).

## 2. Return opportunities (`return-cadence.json`)

**Data.** The same GCAT snapshot. Phase records come from `satcat*`, `auxcat`, `lprcat`, `tmpcat`, `csocat` and the extra-phase file `ecat` (7,500 later phases such as undock and landing). In total 10 files, 99,941 records and 27.6 MB.

**Method.**
1. A return event is a payload-type object (`Type` P*) whose phase ends with status `L` (landed) on primary Earth; one event per object per day. Cargo inside capsules (`Type` C*) is counted separately as down cargo. Suborbital flights (`lcat`) and launch aborts (`ftocat`, e.g. Soyuz MS-10) are excluded.
2. Each landed vehicle is classed from its GCAT bus and name: Dragon cargo (CRS naming) vs Crew Dragon; Soyuz; Shenzhou; Starliner; Varda reentry vehicle; SpaceX Starfall; X-37B/CSSHQ; Chinese recoverable satellites; other capsules and demos (Bion-M, Foton, IXV, LOFTID, HSRC, ATMOS Phoenix); Shuttle; Russian film capsules; deep-space returns (Orion, sample capsules); Starship test ships.
3. **US-accessible** (our classification, a judgement) = Dragon cargo, Crew Dragon, Starliner, Varda and Starfall: vehicles a US commercial payload could plausibly ride home. **Commercial-downmass role** = Dragon cargo and Varda.
4. Gaps = time between consecutive landings within a group. Window: 1 Jan 2021 (Dragon-2 era) to 24 Oct 2026. 2012–2020 is kept for comparison.
5. Mean wait for a product finished at a uniformly random time = Σgap² / (2 Σgap). This is the inspection paradox: long gaps catch more products. The fleet model's equivalent is a uniform wait of cadence/2 (12 h at 24 h).
6. Down cargo = GCAT objects named "… Down Cargo". A final status of `AL`* means it landed inside a capsule (returned); `AR`* means it re-entered with a disposal vehicle (burned). GCAT's coverage is continuous only from 2017: SpX-10 to 34, Progress MS-1 to 33 (MS-04 was a launch failure), Cygnus NG-10 to 21 and Tianzhou 2 to 9 have no gaps. The chart therefore starts in 2017, and the comparison uses 2021 onward.
7. Integrity check: mission numbers must run without gaps. Landed CRS 1–34 is missing only CRS-7 (2015 launch failure); Soyuz MS 1–28 is missing only MS-10 (2018 abort, excluded by design); Shenzhou 2–22 is complete. An external Wikipedia cross-check was attempted, but the Wikimedia API rate-limited our IP.

**Results (2021-01-01 to 2026-10-24, 5.81 years).**

| Group | Landings | Per year | Median gap | p25–p75 | p90 | Longest | Mean wait (random finish) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All LEO capsules, world | 69 | 11.87 | 23.1 d | 12.3–41.8 d | 68.9 d | 129.8 d | 26.1 d |
| US-accessible | 42 | 7.23 | **45.3 d** | 19.5–71.8 d | 107.1 d | 158.7 d | 38.7 d |
| All Dragon | 33 | 5.68 | 54.3 d | 39.8–90.9 d | 110.1 d | 158.7 d | 42.8 d |
| Commercial-downmass role (Dragon cargo + Varda) | 19 | 3.27 | 82.0 d | 69.2–167.3 d | 215.0 d | 249.4 d | 76.8 d |
| Dragon cargo (CRS) | 14 | 2.41 | 143.6 d | 110.2–177.1 d | 226.8 d | 278.1 d | 87.3 d |
| Varda W-series | 5 | 0.86 | 185.1 d | — | — | 371.7 d | 136.9 d |
| Fleet model, per capsule (synthetic; 3 staggered capsules) | — | 365–730 per capsule | 0.5–1 d | — | — | — | 0.25–0.5 d |

- The median gap between US-accessible return opportunities is **45.3 days, 45× the fleet model's 24 h**. Even pooling every LEO capsule in the world, it is 23 days.
- Only 19 of the 69 landings (3.3 a year) were vehicles whose job includes commercial downmass.
- Down cargo since 2021: about **26.2 t returned** inside capsules, mostly Dragon (24 returned "SpX-n Down Cargo" objects, nominal median 1,925 kg); **58.1 t burned** on purpose in Progress, Cygnus, Tianzhou and HTV-X.
- Commercial capsule re-entries *not* recorded as landed: Varda W-4 reentry vehicle (status R, 2026-06-01) and The Exploration Company's Mission Possible (status D, 2025-06-24). Varda W-7, W-8 and W-9 were in orbit at the cutoff.
- New in 2026: two **SpaceX Starfall** landings (Starfall Demo, 23 Jun; Starfall 1, 20 Oct). GCAT does not state their payload purpose.

**Calibration for `analysis/fleet.py`.** `return-cadence.json → calibration` holds, for each group, `n_returns`, `returns_per_year`, the median, mean and p10/p25/p75/p90/min/max gap in hours, the mean wait for a random finish, and the full empirical `gaps_hours` list, so the model can sample gaps instead of using a fixed cadence. Suggested settings: baseline `us_accessible` (median 1,086 h), conservative `dragon_cargo` (3,447 h) and optimistic `all_leo_capsules` (555 h). The 2012–2020 era is included for comparison (US-accessible median 110 days then).

**Limitations.** Some landing times are flagged uncertain or are day-precision only, which moves gaps by hours, not weeks. "US-accessible" and "commercial downmass" are classifications, not manifests. A landing is only an opportunity if the payload was booked on it, so pooled gaps overstate real access. Crew-vehicle downmass is not catalogued separately. Down-cargo masses are nominal. Varda and Starfall have too few flights for a stable rate. The last gap is right-censored.

## 3. Demand signals (`demand-signals.json`)

**Data.**

| Source | Records | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ClinicalTrials.gov bulk download (`ctg-studies.json.zip`, API v2) | 608,415 studies, 0 parse errors | 2.77 GB zipped, 10.53 GB of JSON | sha256 `004e182c…cbc3a`. 464,253 interventional; 25,470 with an INN "-mab" antibody |
| FDA Purple Book (Sep 2026) | 2,251 product rows | 487 KB | 561 antibody rows in 250 BLAs |
| openFDA Drugs@FDA bulk | 29,416 records | 9.2 MB | Approval-date cross-check: 243 of 250 CDER antibody BLAs found, 242 with the same date |
| USGS DS140 lithium xlsx, plus MCS 2022/2023/2024 lithium CSVs and the MCS 2026 commodities table | 8,886 rows (MCS 2026 table) | ≈3.2 MB | World production, lithium content |

**Method.**
1. The full registry was downloaded on Engaging and its 608,415 study files were streamed inside the zip, without extracting, by 6 processes (`mit_quicktest`).
2. Only interventional studies were kept. An antibody intervention is one whose name or other names contain an INN "-mab" word, "monoclonal antibody" or "mAb". A biologic is an intervention of type BIOLOGICAL, or an antibody.
3. Route text is attributed to the antibody itself: its name, other names and description, plus text windows around its name in arm descriptions and titles. Each window is cut at the next mention of another intervention, so a co-drug's route is not credited to the antibody.
4. Primary definition: full words, SC/IV/SQ in a dosing context, s.c., i.v., subQ, and "infusion" for IV (excluding subcutaneous infusions and infusion reactions). Sensitivity runs use full words only, and a crude whole-trial match.
5. Each study counts once, by start year. SC share = studies with an SC antibody route ÷ studies stating any SC or IV antibody route, with Wilson 95% intervals. 2026 is partial and includes planned starts.
6. A hand check used a fixed hash-based sample: 20 SC-positive and 20 IV-positive studies.
7. Hyaluronidase co-formulation and SC brand names (Faspro, Hybreza, Qvantig, Qlex, Zunovo, Phesgo, MK-3475A) give a high-precision marker of IV-to-SC programmes.
8. Purple Book: each antibody BLA is dated by its first approval and the routes licensed that day. For 351(a) products, the first IV and the first SC licence of each antibody are found and cross-checked against Drugs@FDA.
9. Lithium: DS140 world production (2000–2021) is joined with the MCS 2022, 2024 and 2026 world totals, with the latest edition winning where they overlap.

**Results.**
- **21 antibodies were licensed IV first and later received an SC 351(a) licence; 16 of them in 2020 or later** (FDA Purple Book; file headline "16"). They include pembrolizumab (Keytruda Qlex, 2025-09-19, BLA 761467), daratumumab, pertuzumab, ofatumumab, ravulizumab, vedolizumab, infliximab (Zymfentra), spesolimab, atezolizumab, ocrelizumab, nivolumab, lecanemab, mosunetuzumab, amivantamab, anifrolumab and isatuximab.
- SC antibody trials grew from **31 studies starting in 2005 to an average of 248 a year in 2021–25 (about 8×)**.
- The **SC share** of route-stating antibody studies was 15% in 2005–09, peaked at 34% in 2014, and was 21% in 2020–25 (95% CI 20–22%). On the full-words-only definition it is 26% and 29%. Part of the dip is the checkpoint-inhibitor IV boom: those drugs went from 2% to 50% of IV antibody mentions between 2005 and 2020. Excluding them, the SC share still fell (40% in 2014 to 30% in 2020).
- New originator antibody licences with an SC product at first approval: 3/20 (1994–2004), 6/25 (2005–14), 25/52 (2015–19), 31/86 (2020–26).
- Hyaluronidase co-formulation, the incumbent route to high-dose SC: 9 licensed antibody products, and 143 ClinicalTrials.gov studies. Merck has 12 of them, all pembrolizumab with berahyaluronidase alfa (e.g. NCT03486873, NCT05017012, NCT05722015, NCT06041802).
- Lithium world production (lithium content, excluding the US): 32,200 t in 2015 to 290,000 t (USGS estimate) in 2025, 9.0×.
- Classifier hand check: 18/20 SC and 19/20 IV calls correct. All three errors gave a co-administered drug's route to the antibody. 38% of sampled antibody studies (208/545) state no route.
- No solid-state or barocaloric cooling demand series: none quick and real was found, and nothing was substituted.

**Limitations.** These are demand *proxies*. Trial counts are not doses, patients or sales, and say nothing about demand for orbit-made crystals. Route comes from free text: precision is about 90–95% and recall was not measured. The "-mab" match misses code-named antibodies and the post-2021 INN scheme, and "biologic" includes vaccines and cell therapies. Start year is not approval year. Registration rules changed in 2005, 2007 and 2017, and studies from one sponsor and molecule are not independent. Purple Book counts licences, not sales. The incumbent high-dose SC technology is hyaluronidase co-formulation; crystalline suspensions are an alternative, and nothing here shows they will win. Lithium is a supply series: 2023 is an MCS 2024 estimate, 2024–25 are estimates, US consumption has been withheld since 2021, and it implies nothing about MOF membranes specifically.

## 4. Literature map and partners (`literature-map.json`)

**Data.** OpenAlex works API (CC0), accessed 2026-10-24, run locally on the Mac. It used 8 `title_and_abstract.search` queries plus 3 `group_by` calls: 4,672 distinct works harvested (4,532 in the six-topic union), 17.1 MB of API responses cached outside the repo. OpenAlex now meters keyless use at $0.10/day per IP. We used **301 credits on the Mac IP and none on the Engaging IP**. No API key was obtained, and the `mailto` is a generic project address (`orbital-foundry-hackathon@users.noreply.github.com`). No personal email was sent.

**Method.**
1. Space terms: microgravity, "micro-gravity", spaceflight, "space flight", "International Space Station", "space station", "reduced gravity". A bare "ISS" was left out because it is ambiguous.
2. Each topic's phrase set (protein crystallization; pharmaceutical crystallization/polymorph, broad and narrow; solidification/alloy; colloid/photonic crystal; metal–organic framework; zeolite as context) was ANDed with the space terms in one search filter, and the boolean parse was checked in `meta.x_query`. "In-space manufacturing" (and variants) was searched on its own.
3. All matches were harvested at 200 per page and deduplicated by work id. Keyless cursor paging returns HTTP 401, so `page=N` was used, which lost 3 of 2,712 solidification works. Yearly counts, the union, institutions (distinct works), companies (institution type `company`) and countries were computed locally. One `group_by` over all OpenAlex works gives a per-million normalisation.
4. Precision: a seeded random sample of 20 titles per topic was judged on-topic or not (all 13 for MOF). All 132 pharma hits were read by hand, with unclear abstracts checked through free single-work lookups.
5. Partners were matched by regex against resolved institution names, and against raw affiliation strings where no institution was resolved. Every match was checked by hand.

**Results.**

| Topic (× space terms) | Works | Spot-check precision |
|---|---|---|
| Solidification / alloy | 2,709 (of 2,712) | 15/20 |
| In-space manufacturing | 978 | 13/20 |
| Protein crystallization | 576 | 14/20 |
| Colloid / photonic crystal | 310 | 12/20 |
| Pharmaceutical (broad) | 119 | 2/20 |
| Pharmaceutical (narrow) | 21 | 5/20 |
| Metal–organic framework | 13 | 3/13 (all read) |
| Zeolite (context) | 141 | 8/20 |

- **Headline:** of 4,532 works pairing microgravity with a foundry process, only **11** concern crystallizing small molecules or drug ingredients in microgravity, and **10 of them are from 2024 or later**. Redwire is on 5 and Butler University on 5 (the PIL-BOX papers). Varda is on 1.
- MOF growth in real or simulated microgravity: 3 records, 2 distinct studies. 9 of the 13 MOF hits are about *using* MOFs aboard spacecraft.
- Top institutions: NASA Marshall 434, NASA Glenn 172, DLR 156, NASA HQ 132, CNRS 112, CAS 99, JAXA 77 (with NASDA), ESA 75.
- Companies are on 354 of 4,532 works (7.8%), a share that is flat across eras (8.4%, 8.0%, 7.2%). Top companies: Maruwa Foods and Biosciences 21 and Confocal Science 17 (both on JAXA protein-crystal papers), Boeing 16, Advanced Engineering Services 13, and Northrop Grumman, Wyle and ZIN 12 each.
- Partner check, affiliated works: Merck & Co. 4 (including the 2019 pembrolizumab paper, W2993431654), Redwire 10, Made In Space 4, Techshot 2, Axiom 4, Nanoracks 2, LambdaVision 1, CASIS/ISS National Lab 4, Eli Lilly 5, BMS 1, Airbus 4, CNES 9. **Zero:** Space Tango, ACME/Astral Materials, Sierra Space (a zero is not absence of activity).
- SpaceX: 0 verified works in the union and 1 in the narrow pharma set. OpenAlex attributes two other works to SpaceX by mistake (excluded by hand).
- Normalised trend: the union fell from 33.0 per million OpenAlex works in the 1990s (Shuttle/Spacelab era) to 10.5 in the 2010s and 12.8 in 2020–25. The recent uptick is mostly in-space manufacturing: 529 works in 2016–25 vs 80 in 2006–15.

**Limitations.** Recall was not measured. Precision ranges from 2/20 to 15/20, with Wilson intervals in `stats.topics`. The relevance judgements are an AI agent's reading of titles and abstracts, not expert review. OpenAlex resolves an institution for only about 70% of works (5,510 of 17,388 authorships have none) and mis-resolves some strings. 2026 is partial. Records include duplicates and corrections. Company counts undercount industry work, much of which is patented or unpublished. Paper counts measure research attention, not demand.

**Reproduce.** `python3 -I analysis/bigdata/logistics_literature.py --raw-dir <cache> --out data/results/bigdata/literature-map.json` costs about 300 credits from an empty cache. Add `--offline` to rebuild from the cache with zero API calls.

## What the website should say, and avoid saying

- **Say:** "SpaceX flew about half of 2025's orbital launches and carried 85% of the payloads." "Since 2021, a US-accessible capsule has landed every 45 days at the median." "Return, not launch, is the bottleneck: our fleet model's 12–24 h return cadence is a design target, about 45× more frequent than today's reality."
- **Say (demand):** "Since 2020 the FDA has licensed subcutaneous versions of 16 antibodies first licensed as IV infusions, Keytruda Qlex among them," and "SC antibody trials grew about 8× since 2005." Hyaluronidase co-formulation is the incumbent high-dose SC route (9 licensed products), and crystalline suspensions would compete with it.
- **Say (literature):** "Only 11 papers address drug crystals in microgravity, 10 of them since 2024." "Only 2 studies grow MOFs in microgravity." "Companies appear on about 8% of papers."
- **Avoid:**
  - Presenting the fleet model's on-time rates as achievable with today's vehicles.
  - Implying Starfall's purpose (GCAT does not state it).
  - Calling Starship "operational" or "orbital" without the GCAT caveat.
  - Reading down-cargo tonnes as manifests.
  - Saying the SC *share* of trials is rising: it peaked in 2014.
  - Saying any of this shows demand for orbit-made crystals, or that lithium growth implies a MOF-membrane market.
  - Calling keyword counts "papers on X" without the precision caveat (pharma and MOF raw hits are mostly off-topic).
  - Citing OpenAlex as evidence that SpaceX publishes in this field.
  - Reading a zero-paper partner (Space Tango, Sierra Space, ACME/Astral) as "no activity".
