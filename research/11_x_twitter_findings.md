**Problems surfaced (orbital compute):**
1. **Where to put thousands of compute satellites** without raising collision risk (Carmack's question). Data: CelesTrak/Space-Track catalogs, atmospheric density, and the solar cycle.
2. **Heat rejection and power per ton** (Musk: <100 kW/ton, GPUs at 370 K). Data: solar irradiance and eclipse fraction per orbit, computed from orbital mechanics.
3. **"Just the results beamed back"**: which onboard workloads, such as Earth-observation inference, make sense versus downlinking raw data. Data: EO archives (Sentinel/Landsat), downlink budgets.
4. **Ground single points of failure** (the Memphis outage).

### D1. Michael Nicolls, SpaceXAI President (ex-Starlink VP Engineering), keynote at IAC 2026 (early October 2026)

Source: a Grok Bot summary of the talk posted by @cb_doge on 2026-10-06 (824k views). [x.com/cb_doge/status/2107575297432572144](https://x.com/cb_doge/status/2107575297432572144) **[commentary summarising an official talk; check against the IAC recording if possible]**

Key claims, paraphrased from the summary:
- **"The biggest risk as constellations grow is not debris. It's satellite operators not sharing data with each other."**
- Starlink has over 11,000 satellites, "about 60% of all active satellites in low Earth orbit", and serves 13M+ customers in about 170 markets.
- Each satellite makes a collision-avoidance move about every 10 days, so the fleet makes **about 1,000 avoidance moves a day**. Starlink's threshold for moving is "nearly 100x more cautious than the industry standard". "Ducking" (turning the satellite to present a smaller target) cuts risk about 10x more.
- Starlink has maneuvered around satellites from **about 650 operators in recent months, but shares position data with only about half of them. "That number is too low."** Many operators don't share, including some in the US, Europe and Japan. SpaceX shares data with China's space station and some Chinese operators.
- "What you can't avoid is a satellite that maneuvers without telling anyone. Starlink has had passes as close as tens of meters this way."
- **Stargaze** uses the 30,000+ star trackers on Starlink satellites to spot passing objects as streaks against the stars. It tracks 17,000+ objects, including about 90% of the maneuvering satellites that cross Starlink's orbits. In one example, a predicted 9 km miss shrank to about 60 m after the other satellite maneuvered; Stargaze caught it in time.
- SpaceX runs a space safety data platform with **100+ operators signed up**. **The ask: "join the platform and share their positions."**
- **Space weather:** during the 2024 solar storm, "air density in orbit jumped 5x in a few hours", which breaks position forecasts. Starlink is "probably one of the best sensors" for this.
- **Astronomy:** a mirror-like film makes a V2 satellite about as bright as "a sheet of paper" (work with Rubin Observatory). Beams are steered around radio telescopes (NRAO).
- **End of life:** deorbits are predicted statistically, and of about 10,000 satellites launched by early 2026 "only a few dead ones remain in orbit."
- **StarMind**, SpaceX's AI satellite constellation:
  - launches start next year
  - about 250 kW per satellite, roughly one NVL72 rack
  - clusters of about 10 satellites linked by about 10 Tbps
  - filed for up to **1 million satellites** in low-inclination and sun-synchronous orbits
  - challenges: reliability, close-formation flying, micrometeoroids; dead satellites might be sent away from Earth instead of burning up
  - 2+ GW of ground compute is being built in Memphis
- "Coordination is a key element for realizing all of our ambitions."
