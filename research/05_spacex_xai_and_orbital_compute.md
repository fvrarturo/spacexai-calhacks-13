## Part B — Starlink: operations, safety and the problems SpaceX talks about

### B1. Starlink constellation size (Oct 2026) and V3 satellites

- **What it is**
  - ~11,100–11,150 Starlink satellites in orbit in October 2026. KeepTrack: 11,131 in orbit, 10,838 working, 12,918 cataloged since May 2019; 11,374 SpaceX-owned in orbit including Starshield ([KeepTrack live count](https://keeptrack.space/live-count/how-many-satellites-does-spacex-have)); HighSpeedInternet.com: ~11,150 on 16 Oct 2026 ([HSI, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.highspeedinternet.com/resources/how-many-starlink-satellites-are-in-orbit-october-16-2026)). SpaceX's Q3 2026 SEC filing reportedly says it ended Q3 with "more than 11,100 Starlink satellites in low Earth orbit" (search-result summary of the Spaceflight Now Starlink 15-15 article; [Spaceflight Now, 20 Oct 2026](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/10/20/live-coverage-spacex-to-launch-27-starlink-satellites-on-falcon-9-rocket-from-vandenberg-sfb-5/) — not opened directly).
  - Growth: ~6,000 (2024) → >10,000 (June 2026), while total operational spacecraft rose ~10,000 → ~16,000; SpaceX has applied to the FCC to expand Starlink to 100,000 satellites. ([Starpath, 15 Jul 2026](https://starpath.global/news/starlink-satellites-now-dodging-collisions-almost-weekly-as-maneuvers-triple-in-a-year/))
  - **V3 Starlink**: ~1 Tbps downlink (~10× V2), ~160 Gbps uplink (~22× V2); a full Starship can carry up to 60 V3s; first 26 V3s deployed on Starship Flight 14 (28 Sep 2026). ([Light Reading, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.lightreading.com/satellite/starship-reaches-orbit-releases-26-high-capacity-v3-starlink-satellites)) V3 mass ~2,000 kg (~2× V2); 2,048 downlink + 2,048 uplink beams vs 192/144 on V2. ([Wikipedia: Starlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink))
  - **Jonathan McDowell's ledger (updated 24 Oct 2026):** 13,042 launched; 20 failed to orbit; 1,872 down (181 early deorbit, 1,519 disposal complete, 152 uncontrolled reentry after failure); **11,170 in orbit; 11,155 working**; 10,022 in operational shell; 121 retiring; **720 "lowered (out of constellation)"**; 59 reserve/relocating; 148 drift; 85 ascent; 15 failed and decaying. By generation: V1.0 1,665 launched (1,089 down); V1.5 2,974 (420 down); V2 Mini 8,282 (269 down); V3 46 launched (26 in orbit; the other 20 were Flight 13's suborbital test sats, intentionally burned up). ([planet4589.org Starlink stats](https://planet4589.org/space/con/star/stats.html); [Wikipedia: List of Starship launches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Starship_launches))
  - Business: **13.6 million consumer subscribers at 30 Sep 2026** (8.9M Dec 2025 → 10.3M Mar 2026 → 12M Jun 2026); ARPU fell from $99 (Dec 2023) to $66 (Mar 2026); 2025 Starlink revenue $11.4B, operating income $4.4B; Q3 2026 Connectivity segment $5.25B revenue / $2.37B operating income. Starlink ≈ 75% of active maneuverable satellites. ([Wikipedia: Starlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink))
- **Data:** CelesTrak Starlink GP/TLE sets and SupGP (operator ephemeris-derived), Space-Track.org, Jonathan McDowell's Starlink stats ([planet4589.org](https://planet4589.org/space/con/star/stats.html)) — per-satellite status table with shell / lowered / decaying flags, KeepTrack.
- **Hackathon angle:** McDowell's per-satellite table + SupGP history = a free, labeled dataset of ~13k satellite life histories (launch → raise → operate → lower → reenter, with failures). Survival analysis / anomaly early-warning ("which satellites are about to fail?") is a well-posed 36-h ML problem directly relevant to Starlink ops (see B10, on-orbit ops job: "automate fault detection and response").
- **Credibility:** tracker sites (secondary, but McDowell is the reference standard) + reputable press.

### B2. Collision-avoidance maneuver explosion (FCC semiannual reports)

- **What it is.** SpaceX must file semiannual Starlink reports with the FCC listing propulsive collision-avoidance maneuvers, failures and deorbits. The trend:
  - Dec 2024–May 2025: **144,404** maneuvers (~1 every 1.8 min) ([FODNews](https://fodnews.com/spacex-s1-ipo-orbital-debris-starlink-unusable/); [NewSpace Economy, 10 Apr 2026](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/04/10/debris-or-destiny-how-megaconstellation-operators-are-rewriting-the-rules-of-orbital-sustainability/?amp=1))

- **Problems / pain points**
  - Threshold: SpaceX maneuvers when Pc > 3 in 10 million (Starpath) — FODNews describes it as a 1-in-1,000,000 threshold, ~100× more conservative than the industry 1-in-10,000 norm. (Numbers differ between sources; both indicate a far stricter threshold than peers.)

  - Other operators "that report erroneous satellite location data or report nothing at all" continue to create operational challenges (SpaceX FCC filing via [Space Intel Report](https://www.spaceintelreport.com/spacex-148696-starlink-collision-maneuvers-in-6-months-ending-nov-30-continued-issues-with-operators-slipshod-reporting/)).

### B4. "Stargaze" — SpaceX's free SSA / conjunction service built from Starlink star trackers (Jan 2026)

- **What it is.** Unveiled 30 Jan 2026: Starlink's ~30,000 star trackers (on ~9,500–9,600 satellites) detect other orbiting objects — "approximately 30 million transits detected daily across the fleet" — aggregated into orbit estimates and fed to SpaceX's space-traffic-management platform, which generates Conjunction Data Messages (CDMs). Claimed "several-order-of-magnitude increase in detection capability compared to conventional ground-based systems" and conjunction screening in minutes vs hours. Free to all operators who share their own ephemerides; closed beta with >12 operators. Example: a non-sharing third-party sat's maneuver cut predicted miss distance from ~9,000 m to ~60 m five hours out; Stargaze detected it and Starlink planned an avoidance burn within an hour. ([Spaceflight Now, 30 Jan 2026](https://spaceflightnow.com/2026/01/30/live-coverage-spacex-starlink-flight-comes-as-it-unveils-new-stargaze-space-situational-awareness-system/); [Via Satellite, 30 Jan 2026](https://www.satellitetoday.com/sustainability/2026/01/30/starlink-to-launch-free-space-situational-awareness-platform/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/spacex-to-launch-new-space-situational-awareness-system-aims-to-give-data-to-competitors-for-free/); [Leonard David](https://www.leonarddavid.com/stargaze-a-collision-avoidance-system-announced-by-spacex/); [SatellitePro ME](https://satelliteprome.com/news/spacex-unveils-stargaze-system-to-improve-satellite-safety-in-leo/))
- **Problems / pain points (critiques):** covers only "low LEO"; "star trackers are not telescopes"; several government experts said it is "not technically capable" of being a one-stop SSA provider; may disrupt commercial SSA firms and Commerce's TraCSS; Richard DalBello (ex-Office of Space Commerce) welcomed it but argued TraCSS "still matters" and a neutral, standards-based backbone is needed. FY2026 budget request sought to end TraCSS. ([Breaking Defense, Jan 2026](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/01/spacex-unveils-stargaze-space-tracking-system/))
- **Data:** Stargaze output itself is for operators (not open). Public analogues: Space-Track, CelesTrak SupGP, CelesTrak SOCRATES.
- **Hackathon angle:** "Who isn't sharing?" — detect unannounced maneuvers of non-Starlink satellites from public TLE history (change-point detection on mean elements), and rank operators by maneuver transparency. This is exactly the failure mode Stargaze was built to catch (9 km → 60 m surprise). Also: a "Stargaze for everyone" prototype — CDM generation from public data for small operators.
- **Credibility:** official SpaceX update (via reputable press) + defense press.

### B8. Reentry pollution — aluminum oxide from burning satellites

- **What it is.**
  - Ferreira, Huang, Nomura & Wang (USC), "Potential Ozone Depletion from Satellite Demise during Atmospheric Reentry in the Era of Satellite Mega-Constellations", *Geophysical Research Letters* (AGU press release 12 Jun 2024): a typical 250 kg satellite (~30% aluminum) makes ~30 kg of aluminum-oxide nanoparticles on reentry; alumina from reentries rose **8-fold 2016→2022** (17 t in 2022; atmospheric aluminum 29.5% above natural); fully deployed constellations → **~360 t/yr** (646% above natural); particles may take **up to 30 years** to settle into the stratosphere, where they catalyze chlorine–ozone reactions without being consumed. Wang: "Only in recent years have people started to think this might become a problem." ([AGU press release](https://news.agu.org/press-release/satellite-megaconstellations-burn-deplete-ozone/); paper: [GRL doi 10.1029/2024GL109280](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2024GL109280) — 403 on fetch)
  - Spacecraft entries were ~3% of meteoroid entries in 2019 and could reach ~40% with megaconstellations (Schulz & Glassmeier 2021); *Science* news "Burned-up satellites are polluting the atmosphere" (23 Jul 2024); Flamm et al. 2024 on "atmosphere-blindness" of controlled-reentry policy. ([Wikipedia: Space sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_sustainability))
  - FODNews describes Starlink's cumulative annual alumina burden as "above five metric tonnes" ([FODNews](https://fodnews.com/spacex-s1-ipo-orbital-debris-starlink-unusable/)) — unsourced figure.
  - Scale-up arithmetic (ours, not from a source): McDowell counts 1,872 Starlinks down so far (B1); V3 sats are ~2,000 kg (8× the 250 kg reference) → if aluminum fraction were similar, ~240 kg alumina per V3 reentry. A 1M-satellite orbital-compute constellation with 5-year lifetimes would mean ~200,000 reentries/yr.
- **Problems:** uncertainty in alumina yield per satellite type; no monitoring network for reentry aerosol; policy currently *rewards* controlled reentry (burn-up) as the "clean" option.
- **Data:** McDowell reentry list (dates, masses by version), Aerospace Corp. reentry database (CORDS), NASA/NOAA stratospheric aerosol campaigns (Murphy et al. 2023, PNAS, on spacecraft-reentry metals in stratospheric aerosol — page returned 403; content not verified here).
- **Hackathon angle:** "Reentry ledger": daily estimate of metal mass injected by Starlink (and other) reentries from public reentry lists × satellite mass × aluminum fraction, with scenario sliders for V3 and Starmind. Clear, quantified, novel public dashboard; sponsor-sensitive but constructive (e.g., "which design choices cut alumina?").
- **Credibility:** peer-reviewed (GRL, via AGU release) + Wikipedia; FODNews figure unverified.
